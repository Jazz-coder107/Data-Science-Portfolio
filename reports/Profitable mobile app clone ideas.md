# Clone a Niche AI Coach, Not Cal AI

The app to clone is a **niche-sport AI technique coach**. Don't build another calorie scanner or prayer-lock app. The template is **Wrestle AI**. An 18-year-old with no coding background built it on the Rork AI app builder for about **$1,100 in total spend**. Rork's own case study says it reached about **$23K MRR and $131,863 in six months**. That figure is self-reported, and it comes from Rork's vendor marketing ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/george-wrestle-ai-case-study)). Move the template to one sport you can personally reach, where a store search finds no dedicated AI coach. Then beat the incumbents on what AI apps keep getting wrong in reviews: give consistent scores instead of random ones, show one honest free result before the paywall, add a drill-and-progress loop so users come back, and price transparently. The backup is a **taper-first quit-vaping tracker** built on the Puff Count template. Puff Count earned about $40K/month through 2024 (self-reported, older data). This is the easiest build in the research and needs no AI. Version 1 of the coach has six screens and no user accounts. You build it in Rork Pro with RevenueCat for payments, one Supabase Edge Function that holds the AI key, and Cloudflare Pages for the legal pages. It costs roughly **$150–$270 to build and publish in month 1**, which leaves about $730–$850. A lean month-1 marketing plan spends about $225 of that. Google requires new personal developer accounts to run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before going live, so Android launches around day 25–35 and iOS launches first. Keep expectations grounded. Only about **4.6% of apps reach $10K/month within two years**, and the median app earns about **$72/month a year after launch**. Hockey AI used the same template on the same builder and made about **$1K/month in its first month** after spending about $1K on influencers. Nearly every revenue figure in this report is self-reported, and most sources were read through search-engine extracts rather than full pages.

## Every revenue number here carries a trust label

Each revenue figure in this report has one of three labels. **Verified** means an independent directory pulled the revenue from the payment provider (TrustMRR, whatsthe.app or ProvenMRR via RevenueCat), or a major outlet reported a sale or company disclosure. **Estimate** means a firm modelled the number: Sensor Tower, Appfigures, AppGoblin, AppMagic, ScreensDesign, newsletters, or Starter Story's own estimates. **Self-reported** means the number came from the founder, a seller, an acquirer or a vendor. That includes dashboards shown on video and builder case studies such as Rork's. Acquirer and vendor figures count as self-reported because nobody audits them, and the sellers and vendors have reasons to inflate them. A Substack critique of one Starter Story "vibe-coded success" video found officially reported totals of about $30–40K over nine months. That is roughly $4K/month, far below what the video implied ([Paweł Brodziński](https://pawelbrodzinski.substack.com/p/vibe-coded-product-success-stories)). "Approximately" in this report means the figure could not be checked against a primary source and needs verifying before you rely on it.

**Most sources were read through search-engine extracts, not full pages.** The research notes record that page fetching failed for nearly every domain. The exceptions were Apple's App Review Guidelines, Apple's Small Business Program page and Anthropic's vision docs, which were read directly. The ponytail rules were also read directly from the local skill file. Prices were checked through extracts on 2026-10-12. Several facts changed during 2026 and could change again. OpenAI shut down Sora; its API ended Sep 24, 2026. Google split its Play fee into service and billing fees on June 30, 2026. Apple acted against Cal AI's paywall in April 2026. Gemini 3.8 Flash's introductory price ends Dec 31, 2026. Whop changed its terms on Sep 3, 2026. TikTok's new Community Guidelines took effect Sep 24, 2026. Data from before 2025 is marked as older.

## Ranked: a niche AI coach first, a quit tracker second

The ranking weighs six things: strength of the revenue evidence, saturation, buildability by a non-developer in one part-time month, running cost, evergreen demand, and whether $1K of mostly organic marketing can move it. One pattern dominates. The categories with the best evidence are also the most crowded. AI calorie scanners are the only category with an **independently verified** solo-scale winner, yet the same directory lists clones earning $20–$52 a month. ### Why the coach wins first place, and the 48-hour gate that can overturn it

The coach beats the quit tracker on four of the user's stated preferences. It was built by a non-coder **with an AI builder** on roughly the same budget. It sits in the only category flagged as underserved. AI is its core feature rather than a bolt-on, which the build specification requires. And its acquisition channel is a community a newcomer can actually join: athletes already film themselves. The quit tracker wins on build simplicity, zero AI cost and evergreen demand, which makes it the right fallback rather than the lead. The coach's weaknesses are real. The evidence is a vendor's marketing. There is no complaint data. Wrestle AI grew through an influencer co-founder. And sport demand is seasonal. Even a habit tracker swings: HabitKit's self-reported revenue runs about $15K/month in summer and about $40K/month in winter ([X](https://x.com/SebastianRoehl/status/2006337735020118284); [Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/sebastian-roehl-habitkit-10k-mrr-25-years)).

So the pick is conditional on a **48-hour validation gate**. It passes only if all of the following hold. A search of both stores for "[sport] AI coach", "technique" and "feedback" finds no dedicated app with meaningful ratings. You can personally reach 15+ testers and 3+ creators or coaches in the sport. The athletes are mostly adults; sending minors' images to third-party AI raises privacy rules this research did not cover. The move's technique is visible in six still frames from a phone clip. The gate also has a quality check on day 3 or 4. Run ten real clips through two cheap vision models. Pass only if the same clip gets the same overall score twice and an experienced athlete or coach judges at least seven of ten fix lists correct. If either check fails, switch to the quit tracker. You keep the RevenueCat setup, legal pages and marketing system, and you drop the AI proxy.

## Tear down competitors from the outside, never the binary

The legal product teardown you chose uses only what any customer can see, buy or read. That is also where the useful information lives. Pricing, onboarding order, paywall timing, retention prompts and review complaints decide outcomes in these niches. A competitor's code does not. Run the checklist below on two or three competitors in about half a day.

| # | Check | How to do it (legal, outside-in) | What to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store listing | Read both store pages and save every screenshot. Note the subtitle or short description, category, ratings count, last update date, the developer's other apps and the in-app purchase price list | Positioning line, keyword guesses, price points |
| 2 | Revenue signal | Check TrustMRR, whatsthe.app or ProvenMRR (verified); AppGoblin, Appfigures, Sensor Tower or ScreensDesign (estimate); founder posts (self-reported) | Number, label and date |
| 3 | Onboarding | Fresh install with screen recording on. Count screens until the first useful result and until the paywall. List every question asked | Steps to value, steps to paywall |
| 4 | Paywall | Screenshot every variant. Note plans, billed prices, trial, auto-renew text, restore button, terms links, close button, and what happens when you decline | Compare against Apple 3.1.2 and Apple's April 2026 action on Cal AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/21/apples-cal-ai-crackdown-signals-its-still-policing-the-app-store/)) |
| 5 | Core loop | Use the free path. If needed, start a trial with your own money and cancel the same day; 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0 anyway ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)) | Time to value, number of taps, quality on 5 test inputs |
| 6 | Retention loops | Keep it installed for 7 days with notifications on. Log every push, streak, reminder and email | Cadence and copy |
| 7 | Review mining | Read the latest 100 reviews rated 1–3★ on each store and tag the themes. JustUseApp-style summaries speed this up | Top 5 complaint themes with counts |
| 8 | Marketing | Search TikTok, Instagram and YouTube for the app name and the niche phrase. Note accounts, formats, hooks and posting frequency. Check TikTok Creative Center and Meta's public ad library for running ads | Hooks to learn from, never to copy word for word |
| 9 | Support | Send one real support question | Hours until a reply |
| 10 | Differentiation memo | Write one sentence on what is "meaningfully different", which is Apple's 4.3(b) test, plus a list of everything you will **not** copy | Go or no-go |

You do not need the REA tool for any of this. REA (github.com/morluto/rea) was described to us as an MCP server for lawful local reverse engineering of binaries, APKs and websites; this research did not evaluate it. Decompiling a competitor yields code you cannot reuse and none of the commercial signals above. Copying is also where store and legal risk concentrates. Apple's guideline 4.1(a) says "Don't simply copy the latest popular app on the App Store, or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." Guideline 4.1(c) bars using "another developer's icon, brand, or product name". Guideline 4.3(b) rejects apps "indistinguishable from what's already widely available". Guideline 5.2 covers third-party IP ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), read directly). The NYT's 2024 DMCA campaign shows that trade dress can trigger takedowns even when the mechanic itself is free to use ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/nyt-dmca-takedown-request-wordle-clones-reactle-1851320538)). Clone the job-to-be-done, the pricing logic and the onboarding order. Never copy code, names, icons, colours, screenshots or copywriting. Never put competitor names in your keyword field, because Apple can reject apps with irrelevant or competitor keywords ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).

## Ponytail cuts v1 to six screens and zero accounts

The ponytail rules come down to three instructions. Take "the smallest version that does the core job". Use what the platform already gives you before adding anything. Name everything you skip in one line. The rules also forbid cutting four things: "validation at trust boundaries, error handling that prevents data loss, security, accessibility". They require one small test for any money or security logic, and a `shortcut: <the limit>, <when to upgrade>` comment wherever a known limit is accepted. This section applies the default "full" level to v1 and the "ultra" level, which questions the request itself, to your proposed stack.

The **core job** in one sentence: *an athlete films one rep of a move and, in about a minute, gets consistent 1–5 scores against a fixed rubric, the top three fixes, one drill for the biggest fix, and a comparison with their last attempt.* That needs six screens: onboarding (three steps), home (record or pick a clip, plus history), analyzing, result, paywall and settings. Everything else waits until paying users ask for it.

What stays, because ponytail never cuts it:
- **Consent before frames go to a third-party AI.** Apple 5.1.2(i) requires "explicit permission".
- **Security and money on the server.** The AI key stays server-side, with a quota and an entitlement check.
- **Input validation at the proxy.** This is a trust boundary.
- **Failures that never burn the free analysis.**
- **Restore purchases.**
- **Live legal URLs.**
- **A "report this result" button.** Google Play's AI-content policy requires it.
- **Accessibility labels and dynamic text.**
- **One self-check test of the quota and entitlement logic.**

The accepted shortcut is that the free quota is keyed to RevenueCat's anonymous user ID, so a reinstall can earn a second free analysis. Abuse is cheap, at about $0.001–$0.02 per analysis, so the code should carry the comment `shortcut: free quota keyed to anonymous ID, reinstall resets it; add device checks if logs show abuse`.

## Google's 14-day tester clock sets the 30-day plan

Two store rules shape the month. First, a **new personal Google Play account must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for the last 14 continuous days** before it can apply for production access. Production stays disabled until then ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). The production-access review time is unverified; third parties cite up to seven days. Second, Apple rejects a lot. A secondary write-up of Apple's data counts 2,093,244 rejections out of 9,100,620 submissions in 2025, about 23%. App-completeness problems were the largest category: crashes, a paywall the reviewer can't reach, dead links ([trysonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-store-rejection-common-reasons-and-fixes)). Apple's review turnaround was not verified, so the plan assumes at least one rejection cycle. Rork pushes signed Android builds to the internal testing track, and you manage the closed track in Play Console ([Rork docs](https://docs.rork.com/faq/publishing)).

## Market the athlete's story, then show the app

### How to talk about the app

Lead with the athlete's problem and outcome. Mention the mechanism second. A workable positioning line is: *"For [sport] athletes who don't get enough one-on-one feedback, [App] turns one filmed rep into three specific fixes and a drill in about a minute."* Show the real app on screen at the story's turning point. TallUp's AI-character videos reached 7.5M views, but the team "still had to show the app more clearly" before downloads caught up ([SGE](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/three-formats-14m-views-tallups-multi-angle-growth-strategy)). Make modest claims ("AI feedback can be wrong; it's a second pair of eyes, not your coach"). Inaccurate AI output is the most common complaint in this category, so modest claims also protect your reviews. Borrow the exact words athletes use in reviews and comments. Make one promise per post. Never name competitors.

### Organic short-form playbook

Organic posting comes first because it shows which messages work before you spend anything ([RevenueCat Sub Club](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/sub-club-podcast-greg-stewart-ladder-fitness-app-growth)). The most comparable one-person case is Visualize AI. It used just **2 faceless TikTok accounts** and reportedly drew 37.6M views and 5K downloads in weeks, using a "help me choose" problem-to-solution hook. That is a third-party write-up from about Oct 2025 ([SGE](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/niche-switch-how-this-ai-app-pulled-40m-views)). Another app, Stronger, made **725 TikTok videos in 295 days (about 2.5 a day)**, and 98% of its high performers were slideshows (third-party) ([Shortimize](https://shortimize.com/blog/the-slideshow-strategy-that-generated-700000-users-in-275-days)). A newsletter later estimated it at about $100K MRR (estimate) ([SGE](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/100k-mrr-with-faceless-slideshows)).

Run **two TikTok accounts plus one Instagram professional account, cross-posting to YouTube Shorts**. Account A covers technique and problems; account B runs a recurring character story. Each account needs its own email or phone. That rule comes from vendor guides, and TikTok's official account limits were not found ([Pixelscan](https://pixelscan.net/blog/can-you-have-multiple-tiktok-accounts/)). Skip the 170–225-account "farms" funded teams use. TikTok makes "unoriginal or reused material" ineligible for the For You feed ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)), and linked accounts can be penalized together.

Slideshows follow a fixed structure: a hook slide under about 8 words that opens a curiosity loop, two or three problem-and-solution slides, the app, then a call to action. When a post underperforms, rewrite **only the hook** and repost, so you know what caused the difference ([Hooked](https://www.hooked.so/blog/how-to-create-a-tiktok-slideshow); [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/faceless-tiktok)). One self-reported analysis from about Sept 2024 found roughly 3% of posts go viral and 10% perform well ([thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1830617819802992989)). On that ratio, judge a format only after 30–100 posts.

Agencies advise putting the search phrase ("[sport] technique feedback") in the on-screen text and the voiceover within the first ~3 seconds; this is agency opinion, not TikTok documentation ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-seo/)). Use Instagram Trial Reels to test risky angles on non-followers ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-new-trial-reels/)). On Reddit, take part genuinely in your sport's subreddits. QUITTR had many accounts blocked for heavy posting ([vc.ru](https://vc.ru/growth/1762617-kak-quittr-dorosli-do-35k-den)). Product Hunt suits productivity tools more than consumer apps ([LaunchList](https://getlaunchlist.com/blog/how-to-launch-on-product-hunt-2026)).

### AI-animated story videos that hook, then hand off to the app

The format comes from viral AI narrative series. Spring 2024 brought "AI Cat Stories" ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-cat-stories-sad-meow-meow-song)). In 2026 came "Fruit Love Island", which Fast Company called TikTok's most popular AI series ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Love_Island)). **No rigorous data shows these beat UGC or slideshows for installs.** AppsFlyer's 2026 analysis of 1.2M creatives still finds UGC-style content winning video spend ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/creative-optimization-report/)). Treat story videos as one of several formats and test them against slideshows on the same angle. Use **stylized, non-human or clearly cartoon athletes**: animal characters follow the cat-story and fruit precedents. Stylized characters sidestep the likeness rules and the stricter labelling that applies to realistic humans.

The workflow takes about 60–90 minutes per video once templated (inference).
1. Pick the week's best-performing angle from the slideshow data.
2. Generate the script with the template prompt.
3. Make a character sheet with front, side and three-quarter views.
4. Generate 5–7 keyframes that reuse the sheet.
5. Animate each keyframe into a 5–8-second clip, taking 2–3 attempts and keeping the best.
6. Add voice.
7. Screen-record the **real app** producing the fix for the turning-point scene.
8. Edit in CapCut: hook text on the first frame within a second, captions, the search phrase on screen and spoken in the first three seconds, the cut to the app recording at the turn, and an end card.
9. Label the video as AI-generated on every platform and post it.
10. Log 3-second hold rate, completion, profile clicks and installs.
11. Make a slideshow of the same angle for comparison.

|---|---|---|
| Underdog arc | 0–2 s hook → 2–10 s setback → 10–18 s low point (the coach has no time) → 18–28 s turn: real recording of the app's three fixes → 28–35 s payoff and call to action | "He lost every match in September." |
| Serialized character | 3–5 episodes, each ending on a cliffhanger. The app appears as the character's "secret" in episode 2 or 3. Same character and voice throughout | "Episode 2: why he keeps getting caught" |
| Mistake → fix | The character repeats one common mistake → freeze-frame → app overlay names the fix → drill demo | "Everyone does this on their [move]." |
| POV | "Your coach has 30 athletes and 2 minutes for you" → the character films one rep → app breakdown | "POV: you finally see your mistake" |
| Day 1 vs Day 14 | Split screen of the character → drill streak → the app's progress screen | "Day 1 vs Day 14 fixing my [move]" |

### UGC, ASO and small paid tests

**UGC.** Nano and micro creators commonly charge a **$70 flat fee per video**, and mid-tier creators charge $100–$300 ([UGC Roster](https://www.ugcroster.com/ugc-statistics)). Usage rights add 25–150% ([Promote.sh](https://promote.sh/blog/content-creator-rate-guide)). Example campaigns in Whop's per-view "Content Rewards" program paid about $0.30–$3 per 1,000 views, but one guide says Whop has set a **$1,000 minimum campaign budget since Sep 3, 2026**. The sources conflict, and that minimum would probably put it out of reach ([FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/whop-clipping-pricing); [Sprites.ai](https://www.sprites.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-guide)). Spend UGC money on two or three athletes or coaches in your sport filming the winning angle. Skip AI avatar "testimonials": photorealistic synthetic people giving reviews carry the heaviest disclosure duties, and US FTC rules on fake testimonials were not researched.

**ASO.** On iOS the app name and subtitle are 30 characters each, and the keyword field is 100 bytes, comma-separated. Don't repeat name words, the word "app", or competitor names ([App Store Connect](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information/)). Google Play allows a 30-character title and an 80-character short description, with no emoji or all-caps ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)). Put the descriptive phrase in the subtitle, for example "[Sport] technique coach". Use the winning hook as the copy on screenshot 1 (inference).

**Paid tests.** Use only the **$100 Apple Ads Basic credit**. You pay per install, with no minimum ([Apple Ads](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/basic)). By my arithmetic from RevenueCat's Health & Fitness medians, an install is worth only about **$1.10 in first-year net revenue** to this app: 6.9% start a trial × 37.7% of trials convert × about $42.49 net on the annual plan ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). The 2025 US median search-ad cost per install was $4.06 ([Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/apple-search-ads-cost)), so scaled paid search loses money until conversion beats the median. TikTok ad groups need more than $20 a day, and practitioners cite $100–$200 a day to exit learning, so skip them ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-ads-minimum-daily-budget-2026)). Adapty says the first $1K on Meta only tests whether creative holds attention ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/first-10k-meta-ads-subscription-apps/)). Boost one proven organic post with $50–$100 at most.

## Most apps never reach $10K a month — verify these first

**Base rates.** RevenueCat's 2026 data, via a secondary write-up, says only **4.6% of apps reach $10K in monthly revenue within two years**, and 17.3% reach $1K ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hard-paywall-vs-free-trial-revenuecat-indie-app-2026)). The median app earns about **$72/month one year after launch** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). AI apps earn 41% more per payer but **churn 30% faster** ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/the-2026-state-of-subscription-apps-report)). Competition from AI-built apps is rising fast:
- Appfigures counted **557K new App Store releases in 2025** ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251205?f=2)).
- App Store submissions rose 84% in one quarter, and Apple is "cracking down" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/06/app-store-sees-84-surge-in-new-apps-as-ai-coding-tools-take-off/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-apple-app-store-surge-crackdown)).
- Naavik found that a 77% jump in game releases produced only a 14% rise in titles earning over $20K ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/is-the-era-of-mobile-ai-slop-games-here/)).

The funnel shows how far $10K MRR is. Assume about $5 a month net per payer, blending the monthly and annual plans after a 15% commission. Then $10K MRR needs about 2,000 active payers. At median download-to-paid rates of 2.4% for AI apps and 10.7% for hard paywalls, that takes **roughly 19,000–83,000 installs, before churn** (my arithmetic). Wrestle AI's self-reported 100K+ downloads at about $23K MRR fits that range.

**Specific risks for the top pick.**
- *AI quality:* inaccurate or inconsistent output is the top complaint in this category.
- *Vendor-marketing evidence:* Rork publishes Wrestle AI, Hockey AI and a third case study, 3AK Track, which Rork says RevenueCat verified at $17,460 in 28 days ([Rork](https://rork.com/blog/3ak-case-study)). Rork has an obvious incentive.

## Conclusion

AI builders have made the build cheap: a non-coder shipped Wrestle AI on Rork for about $1,100, and Prayer Lock was reportedly built in three days. That same cheapness is why App Store releases surged while the number of titles earning real money barely moved. **Code is no longer the moat.** A newcomer's defensible edges are things a weekend clone can't copy: belonging to an audience (a sport community you can reach in person) and earning trust (consistent results, value before the paywall, prices users can read, support that answers). That is the real reason to pick an underserved niche over a better-funded category, and why the "better" version should be judged on its 1–3★ reviews rather than its feature list.

The funnel arithmetic also changes the question to ask at the end of the month. Whether the app works is the easy part. What matters by day 30 is whether you have a repeatable source of **thousands of installs** and a paid conversion rate near or above about 26 payers per 1,000 installs, which is RevenueCat's median Health & Fitness trial-start rate multiplied by its median trial-to-paid rate. Set a decision rule before launch. At day 60, keep going if organic posts reliably produce installs and conversion is at or above that median. Otherwise, move the same stack, paywall and content system to the backup app or a second sport. Most of the month-1 work carries over either way.
