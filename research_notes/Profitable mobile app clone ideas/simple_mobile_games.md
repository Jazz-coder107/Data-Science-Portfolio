## Q1. Which simple mobile games by solo/small developers earn ~$10k+/month, and what is the evidence?

### Takeaway
Hard, recent (2025-2026) evidence of *solo* mobile games at $10k+/month is scarce. The strongest recent case is **Harpagia**, a text-based idle RPG built by one former Meta manager with AI tools. It reports about $13k/month, but that figure is self-reported. The other cases are either web games (fly.pieter.com, Online Solitaire), older hits (Flappy Bird, Geometry Dash, Wordle), or small studios that became very large (Arrows by Lessmore). The big 2025 casual hits (Block Blast, Pixel Flow, Magic Sort) are useful genre benchmarks but are not small-team outcomes.

**1. Harpagia (the most relevant recent case: solo dev, AI tools, simple text UI)**
- Starter Story: Ken spent 12 years in big tech (Amazon, then engineering manager at Meta) and "hadn't written a line of code in 4 years" before AI tools "clicked". He first shipped a tiny card game in 6 weeks just to learn how to launch. — [Starter Story on X](https://x.com/starter_story/status/2102414228464538002) (via search summary; X not directly readable)
- Self-reported metrics: ">$13K/month average, $155K in year one, 50,000+ downloads across iOS + Android, 6% conversion (games average 2–5%), $500/month in costs, $0 on ads." — [Starter Story on X](https://x.com/starter_story/status/2102414228464538002)
- Growth trigger: six months in, he made his first Reddit post (screenshots plus a note on how free-to-play the game was). Starter Story says that single post took revenue from ~$1K/month to ~$10K/month "almost overnight". — [Starter Story on X](https://x.com/starter_story/status/2102414228464538002); [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/this-reddit-post-replaced-a-155k-job)
- Monetization: optional IAPs priced $4.99-$9.99; reportedly >$3 per active user on average; $500/month operating costs (assets, freelance art, subscriptions). — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-13k-game-built-for-a-few-hundred-fans)
- Scale conflict: Stork says ~$150k/year is sustained by "a mere 500-600 daily active users". Another Stork piece says 3,500-4,000 monthly active users. The two counts are inconsistent. — [Stork.ai (13k)](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-13k-game-built-for-a-few-hundred-fans); [Stork.ai (Reddit)](https://www.stork.ai/blog/this-reddit-post-replaced-a-155k-job)
- Tech stack: a podcast summary says Ken used AI tools "like Cursor, React Native, and Firebase". The same summary misspells the game's name, so it is low-reliability. — [BigGo podcast summary](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/4d8f6f4979968082)
- Genre: a text-based idle RPG "designed for fans of incremental games like Runescape", focused on offline progress and deep character customization. — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/the-13k-game-built-for-a-few-hundred-fans); store listings: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/harpagia-offline-idle-rpg/id6738149458), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.harpagia)

### Inferences
- The best-evidenced recent solo pattern is **a niche, community-driven game with IAP and a high ARPDAU** (Harpagia: if the 500-600 DAU figure is right, that is ~$0.70-0.85 ARPDAU, my arithmetic). That is very different from the ad-volume model (Block Blast, Arrows, Cookingdom), which needs millions of DAU and paid UA. A $1k-budget newcomer is far better placed for the first pattern.
- Ad-only casual games need roughly 100k+ DAU to clear $10k/month at typical casual ad ARPDAU (see Q4: ~$0.03-0.16). That arithmetic explains why the ad-driven hits are publisher- or UA-driven, or lucky viral outliers (Flappy Bird).
- Several "evergreen" solo successes (Wordle, Online Solitaire, Clues by Sam, fly.pieter) started on the **web**, not the stores. Web-first validation followed by a store wrapper is a recurring path.
- Small studios can still break out with a single 2D logic mechanic (Arrows), but the reported scale ($250k/day) implies heavy UA plus acquisition by Miniclip. It is not a solo-without-budget outcome.

### Gaps
- I found no Appfigures, Sensor Tower or AppMagic revenue estimate for any **2025-2026 solo-developer mobile game** in the $10k-$100k/month band. That band is under-covered by public sources, which focus on top grossers.
- Harpagia's launch date, store-analytics confirmation, and exact AI tooling are unconfirmed. Starter Story is a media business promoting its own interviews, and the X post could not be read directly.
- No revenue was found for Idle Slayer, The Tower, Retro Bowl or Clues by Sam. The BitLife figures are unusable.
- No reliable 2025-2026 Reddit (r/gamedev, r/iOSProgramming) revenue posts surfaced in search (Reddit results did not appear).

## Q2. Viral indie and AI-made mobile games of 2025-2026: what went viral, why, and did they monetize?

### Takeaway
The viral successes of 2025-2026 fall into two groups: **publisher-scaled hybridcasual puzzles** driven by paid UA (Pixel Flow, Arrows, Block Blast, Solitaire Associations Journey), and **web-first indie/AI curiosities** driven by social sharing (fly.pieter.com, Clues by Sam). I found **no AI-built game that topped the App Store charts**. The data instead shows an AI-driven flood of releases, with little revenue upside per title.

### Cited Findings
- **AI release flood.** Appfigures (via TechCrunch): worldwide app releases in Q1 2026 were up 60% YoY across both stores and 80% on iOS. A separate report counts 181,000 new mobile games in the six months to May 2026 (+118% YoY on iOS, +73% on Android). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260701-mobile-game-boom/)
- **More releases, little more revenue.** Naavik: a 77% jump in game releases (Dec 2025-Feb 2026) produced only a 14% rise in titles earning >$20k. Naavik also says it does not see obvious signs of AI-accelerated growth, since releases have grown roughly linearly since 2022, and that games with essentially no downloads or revenue are the vast majority both before and after the influx (published Apr 19, 2026). — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/is-the-era-of-mobile-ai-slop-games-here/)
- **Attribution and Apple's response.** Sensor Tower data (via 9to5Mac) points to vibe-coding tools as the main contributors to the surge in new apps. A report via The Information says App Store submissions rose 84% in one quarter, and Apple is "cracking down". — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/06/app-store-sees-84-surge-in-new-apps-as-ai-coding-tools-take-off/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-apple-app-store-surge-crackdown)
- **No AI-built chart-topper.** One opinion piece says there is no commercially significant shipped game built primarily on Lovable. — [Sorceress.games](https://sorceress.games/blog/lovable-vibe-coding-revenue-400m-arr-but-not-for-games)
- **fly.pieter.com (Feb 2025).** It went viral on X because of the build-in-public story ("built in hours with AI") and Levels' large following. It monetized through sponsor ad slots, not players; see Q1 for the conflicting figures. — [404 Media](https://404media.co/this-game-created-by-ai-vibe-coding-makes-50-000-a-month-yours-probably-wont); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l)
- **Clues by Sam (2025).** It spread by word of mouth, social media and a Show HN post, using a Wordle-style shareable emoji/text/image result. The design hook is a constraint solver that prevents guessing; the developer said the level generator was most of the work. It monetizes through paid puzzle packs, with no disclosed revenue. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_By_Sam); [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43923843); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/clues-by-sam-wordle-daily-puzzle-game/); [Dave Rupert](https://daverupert.com/2025/11/clues-by-sam/)
- **Harpagia (2025-2026).** It grew virally through one Reddit post that emphasized how free-to-play the game was, and monetizes through IAP at $13k/month (self-reported). — [Starter Story on X](https://x.com/starter_story/status/2102414228464538002)
- **Pixel Flow (Aug 2025).** Players spend close to an hour a day across ~10 sessions. The search summary also said the team reported seven figures in *daily* revenue three months after launch; I could not tie that claim to a specific source. The studio was acquired for >$1B. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream); [AppMagic H1 2026](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-H12026/?hl=en)

(less reliable figures). — [NextBigGames](https://nextbiggames.com/2026/05/30/hybrid-casual-puzzle-trends-2025/); [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/arrows-puzzle-escape-by-lessmore-is-rocking/)
- **Solitaire Associations Journey (Hitapps, Sept 2025).** It combines solitaire with word-association puzzles, held #1 in downloads in its launch month, and peaked at #93 top-grossing by late November (GameRefinery; the market was not specified in the summary). — [GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/mobile-game-market-review-november-2025/)

- **Vibe-coding apps themselves.** A Sept 2025 TechCrunch piece citing Appfigures found that dedicated *mobile apps for vibe coding* had failed to gain traction. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/dedicated-mobile-apps-for-vibe-coding-have-so-far-failed-to-gain-traction/)

### Inferences
- Virality without a monetization design does not last: fly.pieter's revenue reportedly fell back, and Wordle was never monetized. Successful virals pair a **share artifact** (an emoji grid or screenshot) with **one clear purchase or retention reason** (puzzle packs, or a daily return habit).
- For a newcomer with no UA budget, the realistic viral channels are community posts (Reddit, as with Harpagia), share grids (Clues by Sam, Wordle), and build-in-public posts on X. Pixel Flow-style UA-driven hits are not replicable on $1k.
- The AI flood raises the bar for discoverability and review scrutiny (Apple 4.3). A "generic clone" is the most exposed category.

### Gaps
- No verified example of an AI/vibe-coded **mobile store** game earning $10k+/month apart from Harpagia's self-reported claim.
- No list of 2026 viral mobile indie games (Jan-Oct 2026) beyond the Pixel Flow and Arrows follow-ons; the search budget ran out.

## Q3. Which casual genres are evergreen, how saturated is each, and which sub-genres or twists still have room?

### Takeaway
The casual market as a whole is flat (casual revenue +0.8% YoY in H1 2025). Growth has moved to **block, sort, screw, merge and arrow puzzles**, but those are now dominated by publishers running paid UA, and very few new launches break out. The best openings for a solo newcomer are **niche, low-art genres where community or habit beats UA**: daily logic puzzles with a share mechanic, text or minimal-art idle/incremental games, and solitaire/word **hybrids with a twist**. Head-on block, sort or match-3 clones are the worst bets.

## Q4. Teardowns of the 4-6 most clone-friendly games: core loop, hooks, sessions, monetization, ad metrics, retention, review complaints

### Takeaway
The most clone-friendly models for a solo, AI-assisted, ~4-week build are: (A) a **daily logic-puzzle bundle** (Clues by Sam / Queens / Wordle model), (B) a **text- or minimal-art idle RPG** (Harpagia model), (C) an **arrow/escape logic puzzle** (Arrows model), (D) a **sort puzzle** with fair monetization (Magic Sort / Pixel Flow-lite), (E) a **block puzzle** with a better ad experience (Block Blast model), and (F) a **solitaire/word hybrid or web-first solitaire** (Online Solitaire / Solitaire Associations Journey). (A) and (B) best fit a $1k budget, because they monetize through IAP or packs and grow through community. (C)-(E) monetize mainly through ads and need volume.

**(A) Daily logic puzzle: Clues by Sam / Queens / Wordle model**
- Core loop: one free puzzle per day plus optional paid packs of 50 puzzles; results shared as emoji grids, text or images. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_By_Sam); [W. Eric Martin](https://www.wericmartin.com/spotting-criminals-is-a-daily-challenge-with-clues-by-sam/)

### Inferences
- **What makes these "addictive"** (synthesis): (A) a daily scarcity cadence plus streaks plus a share grid; (B) offline progress and number-go-up with long-horizon goals; (C)-(E) short, near-miss levels (5-slot buffer, "one more move", revive prompts) designed around a failure moment that is monetized. Pixel Flow's ~10 sessions a day suggests sessions of about 5-6 minutes (my arithmetic from ~60 min/day).
- **Core loops not covered by my sources (general knowledge, unverified here):** Arrows: tap an arrow and it slides off the board in its direction if the path is clear; clear all arrows. Block Blast: drag three given polyomino pieces onto a grid and clear full rows or columns; no rotation. Please verify against the store listings before the report repeats these.
- **How a better clone could fix the complaints:** (1) a visible, cheap, one-time "Remove Ads" IAP; (2) no interstitial after a failure, with rewarded ads opt-in only; (3) frequency caps, e.g. an interstitial at most every N levels or minutes; (4) restart for free, with revive as a paid or rewarded option; (5) honest store creatives. These directly target the Block Blast review themes.
- **Revenue math for a newcomer:** at ~$0.05-0.10 ad ARPDAU, $10k/month needs ~3,300-6,700 DAU *every day*. With median D7 retention of ~4%, that implies thousands of new installs per day. At a Harpagia-like IAP ARPDAU of ~$0.70-0.85, ~400-500 DAU suffices. **IAP-first niche designs are far more reachable on $1k.**
- **4-week build fit (my ranking):** A (daily logic) ≈ F-web (solitaire) > B (text idle) > C (arrows) > D (2D sort) > E (block). The hard parts are A's puzzle generator/solver, B's balance and economy tuning, and C/D's level-design volume.

- No public ARPDAU, eCPM or retention numbers for the specific small titles (Harpagia, Clues by Sam, Queens clones, Arrows). Session-length data was found only for Pixel Flow.
- I could not run the planned searches on 1-3★ reviews for **sort puzzles, word games, Queens clones or idle RPGs** because the search budget ran out. Only Block Blast's review complaints are sourced.
- Puzzle-specific retention benchmarks are unattributed. The GameAnalytics genre split sits behind a lead-capture form.

## Q6. Store-policy and legal risks of cloning games

### Takeaway
Cloning a *mechanic* is common and broadly tolerated (genres such as sort, block and arrow puzzles are full of near-identical games). Copying a **name, brand, icon or distinctive trade dress** (e.g., "Wordle", Wordle's green/yellow/grey tiles, "Queens" with LinkedIn references) creates real takedown and rejection risk. Apple's 4.1/4.3 copycat and spam rules plus the 2026 vibe-coding crackdown mean a generic, undifferentiated clone is the most likely to be rejected.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 4.1 (Copycats):** "Don't simply copy the latest popular app on the App Store, or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." 4.1(b): impersonating other apps can lead to removal from the Developer Program. 4.1(c): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." — [Apple App Review Guidelines (read directly)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 4.3 (Spam):** 4.3(a) bars multiple Bundle IDs of the same app. 4.3(b): "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery…" The named well-established categories (dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, fortune telling) do **not** include games, but the general "indistinguishable" test applies. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 5.2 (IP):** don't use third-party trademarks, copyrighted works or patented ideas without permission, or "misleading or copycat names or metadata". **4.2.6:** applies to template and app-generation services (see Q5). — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **NYT vs Wordle clones (March 2024, OLD but relevant):** the NYT filed DMCA notices citing its ownership of the Wordle name and look and feel, including the green/grey/yellow tile scheme. GitHub was asked to remove the "Reactle" repo and its forks (reported at 1,900+). The NYT said it has no issue with similar games that avoid its "trademarks or copyrighted gameplay", and developers of non-English variants feared collateral takedowns. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/03/13/new-york-times-wordle-clones-copyright-knock-offs); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/nyt-dmca-takedown-request-wordle-clones-reactle-1851320538); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/the-new-york-times-is-cracking-down-on-wordle-clones-100004668.html)
- **LinkedIn Queens clones:** at least one store app explicitly describes itself as "the same puzzle as the daily Queens puzzle published on LinkedIn". I found no reports of LinkedIn enforcement, but none of absence either. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brennerd.grid_puzzle.queens&hl=en_US)
- **Vibe-coding surge:** App Store submissions rose 84% in a quarter and Apple is "cracking down" (details not read). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-apple-app-store-surge-crackdown)
- **Industry framing:** a Global Games Forum session discussed "gray zone" creatives and copycats in mobile (title only seen). — [Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/gray-zone-creatives-copycats-monetization-misconceptions)

### Inferences
- Safe-cloning checklist: an original name and icon (no "Wordle", "Queens", "Block Blast" or "Pixel Flow" in the title or keywords); original colour scheme and tile styling; at least one meaningful twist (a new rule, a hybrid of two genres, a daily mode, an accessibility or ad-fairness promise) so the 4.3(b) "meaningfully different" bar is clearly met; one app with modes rather than many near-duplicate apps (4.3(a)).
- Generic puzzle mechanics (sorting, sliding, Sudoku-like constraints, N-queens-style placement) are long-standing and widely cloned. The legal risk sits in branding and trade dress, not the abstract rules. This is not legal advice; a trademark search in the target markets is cheap insurance.
- The vibe-coding crackdown probably means more 4.3 rejections for template-looking apps in 2026. A polished, distinct UI and an honest description reduce the risk.

### Gaps
- **Google Play** policies (Impersonation, Spam/Minimum Functionality, Intellectual Property) were not researched; the search budget ran out.
- I found no 2025-2026 examples of Apple rejecting specific puzzle-game clones, and no details of Apple's 2026 vibe-coding enforcement actions.
- LinkedIn's trademark position on "Queens", "Tango", "Zip" and similar game names is unknown.
