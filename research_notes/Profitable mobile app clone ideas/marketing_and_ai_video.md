## 1. Organic short-form playbook (slideshows, POV/story hooks, problem-agitate-solution, multi-account posting, volume, hooks) and documented apps that grew this way

### Takeaway
In 2025-2026, the documented low-budget playbook for consumer apps is high-volume, faceless short-form content. The main formats are TikTok photo-mode slideshows plus Instagram Reels. Successful apps post across several to hundreds of niche "theme" accounts, iterate on the first-slide hook, and show the app late in the post as the solution. Almost every revenue and view number for these apps is self-reported or a newsletter estimate. The one app with press coverage, Cal AI, grew mainly through paid influencers rather than faceless accounts.

### Cited Findings
**Why organic first (practitioner advice)**
- Greg Stewart (Ladder) advises starting with organic content because organic is the leading indicator of winning content. Ad money should wait until organic has shown which messages work. He also notes that TikTok's For You page needs content that lands with *new* viewers every time, unlike Instagram's follower-centric model. — [RevenueCat Sub Club: Greg Stewart / Ladder](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/sub-club-podcast-greg-stewart-ladder-fitness-app-growth)
- Ladder went from 0 to $1M ARR through micro-influencer partnerships on Instagram. RevenueCat credits part of its 500% subscriber growth in 2023 to TikTok (older info, 2023-2024; self-reported). — [RevenueCat Sub Club: Ladder](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/sub-club-podcast-greg-stewart-ladder-fitness-app-growth)
- Joseph Choi (Viral App Founders) argues that TikTok's structure lets apps break through organically, especially with trending topics and relatable content. The episode headline quotes him: "With TikTok, good quality content can go viral from scratch." (2024, older.) — [RevenueCat Sub Club: Joseph Choi](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/joseph-choi-sub-club-podcast-2024)
- A RevenueCat Meta Ads masterclass summary says organic traction on TikTok or Reels is a strong signal that you have an onboarding flow and an angle you can amplify with paid ads. — [Marcus Burke summary of RevenueCat Meta Ads Masterclass](https://marcuscnslt.substack.com/p/revenuecat-meta-ads-masterclass)

**Slideshow / faceless format mechanics (vendor and practitioner guides, not tested data)**
- Typical structure: a hook slide, then value slides, then a CTA. App-specific versions open with a problem stated in one sentence, use 2-3 slides to show how the app solves it, and end on a download CTA. — [Hooked: TikTok slideshow that converts](https://www.hooked.so/blog/how-to-create-a-tiktok-slideshow); [Reels Farm: 11 slideshow ideas for apps](https://reelsfarm.com/blog/11-tiktok-slideshow-ideas-for-marketing-apps); [Slizo faceless playbook](https://www.slizo.io/blog/faceless-tiktok-marketing-playbook)
- Hook rules from these guides: keep the first slide under about 8 words, phrase it as a statement that opens a curiosity loop, and prefer simple over clever. Use concrete claims (a number of reasons, a named mistake). On slideshows, the first slide's text carries the hook and audio is mostly background. If a post underperforms, rewrite only the hook and repost so you isolate the cause. Guides disagree on length: 5-10 slides vs. 7 as default vs. 6-slide templates. — [Cinerads faceless TikTok](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/faceless-tiktok); [Slidestorm idea bank](https://www.slidestorm.ai/articles/tiktok-slideshow-ideas-for-faceless-accounts-the-ultimate-niche-by-niche-idea-bank); [Renderfire 50 slideshow ideas](https://renderfire.com/resources/saas/tiktok-content-ideas)
- Visualize AI, a solo founder's relaunched AI interior-design app, reportedly hit 37.6M views and 5K downloads in weeks with just **2 faceless TikTok accounts**. The top video used a "help me choose" hook over a plain room photo, then quick reveals of AI styles. The newsletter describes this problem-to-solution format with trending audio as working on TikTok "right now" (article about a year old, ~Oct 2025; third-party write-up). — [Social Growth Engineers: Niche Switch / Visualize AI](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/niche-switch-how-this-ai-app-pulled-40m-views)

**Multi-account ("account farm") practice and platform risk**
- TikTok's official Integrity & Authenticity policy makes "unoriginal or reused material with nothing new added" ineligible for the For You feed. It also prohibits spam and fake engagement. — [TikTok Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity); [TikTok newsroom: countering deceptive behaviour](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-eu/how-tiktok-counters-deceptive-behaviour)
- TikTok's Creator Academy originality policy lists as unoriginal: content copied from others; content repurposed without creative edits; compilations with little added value; and content with another platform's watermark. — [TikTok Creator Academy: originality policy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy). Being ineligible for the For You feed is not removal, and creators can appeal from analytics. — [TikTok FYF eligibility standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update)
- I found no official TikTok text on how many accounts one person may run. Vendor guides (which sell multi-account tools) say multiple accounts are allowed if each has a unique email or phone. They say the risk comes from accounts linked by device, IP, duplicate content or ban evasion: one violating account can drag down linked accounts. These are unverified vendor claims, and per-device limits quoted (3 vs. 5 vs. 3-6) conflict. — [Pixelscan](https://pixelscan.net/blog/can-you-have-multiple-tiktok-accounts/); [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/how-to-run-multiple-tiktok-accounts-without-bans/)

### Inferences
- For a solo founder with about $1K, the evidence points to: (1) 2-5 niche faceless accounts (TikTok plus Instagram cross-posts, YouTube Shorts as a third outlet); (2) slideshows as the cheapest format to make in volume; (3) a disciplined hook-testing loop. A 170-225 account "farm" is a funded-team tactic and adds ban-linkage risk. The Visualize AI case (2 accounts) and Stronger (~2.5 posts/day) are the closest analogues to a one-person operation.
- The ratios in the thread (~3% of posts viral, ~10% high-performing) suggest you need roughly 30-100 posts before you can judge a niche or format. That is an inference from one self-reported thread, not a benchmark.
- Survivorship bias is heavy: newsletters cover the winners. The failure rate of this playbook is undocumented.
- For a **casual game**, I found no documented 2025-2026 solo mobile-game case grown by faceless slideshows. Gameplay-clip and "satisfying" video formats are the likely analogue, but that is unverified here.

### Gaps
- No primary-source (RevenueCat dashboard, App Store Connect screenshot) verification for any slideshow app's revenue. Stronger, Fig, Turbo AI and AbMax numbers are newsletter or tracker estimates.
- Could not read the original @iamgdsa thread or the Larry X article in full, so per-account posting cadence, warm-up periods and exact hook lists are unconfirmed.
- No reliable source on "account warm-up" length or method. Only vendor blogs mention it.
- No documented solo casual mobile game grown mainly by short-form in 2025-2026 (a search for one turned up only PC/Roblox examples, e.g. [Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot), which is not a solo dev).

## 2. AI-animated story ads: what the format is, who uses it, evidence of performance, tool stack and current pricing

### Takeaway
The "AI-animated short story with the app as the payoff" format grew out of viral AI narrative series: AI cat stories (2024), "Fruit Love Island" (2026), and Pixar-style shorts. Apps are now borrowing it, e.g. TallUp's AI characters explaining the body. I found no rigorous performance data showing it beats UGC or slideshows. Large creative datasets (AppsFlyer 2026) still find UGC-style video winning spend. The tool stack is cheap: roughly $10-$50/month for one video model plus voice. OpenAI's Sora is no longer an option because it shut down in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**What the format is / where it comes from**
- "Fruit Love Island": Fast Company (Mar 2026) described it as TikTok's most popular AI-generated series. — [Wikipedia: Fruit Love Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Love_Island). The Daily Dot says the cheating-fruit trend started in late Feb 2026 and one installment passed 26M views. — [Daily Dot](https://lede-v2.dailydot.com/ai-slop-videos-of-cheating-fruit-are-taking-over-tiktok)
- Earlier precedent: "AI Cat Stories / Sad Meow Meow Song" (spring 2024) used AI images of cats in sad human-style scenarios and drew millions of views (older). — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-cat-stories-sad-meow-meow-song)
- A widely shared 2025 TikTok shows a one-person Pixar-style animated short: Midjourney characters, Pixverse animation, Kling lip-sync, Udio score. — [TikTok @itsbetterwithai](https://www.tiktok.com/@itsbetterwithai/video/7511436722228497695)
- App use: TallUp AI (height/fitness app) used **AI-generated characters explaining how the body works in relation to height**. The top video hit 7.5M views, but the write-up says the team still had to show the app more clearly so downloads could catch up. — [SGE: TallUp three formats, 14M views](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/three-formats-14m-views-tallups-multi-angle-growth-strategy)
- Studio blog: Pixar-style AI ads are character-led spots for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and Snapchat, and AI removes costs that used to run to hundreds of thousands of dollars. Its claim of higher engagement than live action is unsupported. — [Tetra Media Hub](https://tetramediahub.com/blog/pixar-style-ads.html)

**Evidence of performance (weak)**

### Inferences
- Minimal stack for a $1K total budget: image model (free Gemini or ChatGPT image tools, or Midjourney Basic $10) for character keyframes, then one video model (Kling Standard ~$9-10/mo, *or* Google AI Pro $19.99 for Veo 3.1 Lite/Fast), then ElevenLabs Starter $6, then CapCut free (or Pro $19.99 for one month). That totals about **$35-$60/month**. Buy one month, batch-produce, then cancel. Avoid stacking Higgsfield, Runway and Hedra at the same time.
- Rough per-clip cost: Veo 3.1 Lite on AI Pro is ~10 credits, so ~100 clips/month (8s each). Kling Standard's 660 credits: clip count unknown, because Kling's native credits-per-clip were not verified. The "10 credits per 5s Kling 3.0 clip" figure is in *Higgsfield's* credit units and does not transfer. A 30-45s story needs ~5-8 clips plus retries, so expect roughly **8-15 finished story videos/month** on the cheapest tiers.
- Script pattern implied by the evidence: a relatable character problem (hook in the first 1-2s with on-screen text), then escalation, then the app shown *explicitly* as the turning point (TallUp's lesson: views without a clear app reveal didn't convert), then a CTA. Pair each story video with a cheap slideshow variant so the same angle gets tested in both formats.
- Avoid "Pixar" or "Disney" in captions, prompts that reproduce named characters, or real-person likenesses. Disney/Pixar IP is actively licensed and policed, and TikTok bans AI depictions of public figures endorsing products (see section 3).

### Gaps
- **No primary performance data** (CPI, CTR, install rate) comparing AI-animated story ads to UGC or slideshows for apps. The Liftoff Mobile Ad Creative Index 2026 and the full AppsFlyer report may contain this but were not readable here.
- No named app with verified numbers that grew mainly on AI-animated *story* ads. The format is visible on feeds, but documentation is anecdotal.
- Hedra, CapCut and Midjourney prices are third-party only. Higgsfield's lineup is inconsistent across sources. Kling's native per-clip credit costs were not verified. Ultra-tier Google prices conflict.
- Vendor commercial-use and licensing terms for Kling, Higgsfield and Hedra outputs were not checked.

## 3. UGC creator programs (per video / per view), AI UGC avatar tools, and platform AI-disclosure rules / flagging risk

### Takeaway
Micro-UGC is affordable at roughly $50-$300 per video from marketplaces, or per-view "content rewards" at about $0.30-$3 CPM via Whop. Even so, $1K covers only a few creators. AI UGC avatar tools (Arcads ~$110/mo, HeyGen from $29/mo, Creatify from ~$19-39/mo) are cheaper per video but raise disclosure duties. TikTok, Meta and YouTube all require labeling *realistic* AI content. TikTok can auto-label C2PA-tagged content, and TikTok bans AI fake endorsements and unauthorized likenesses outright.

### Cited Findings
**Human UGC rates (vendor and marketplace data)**
- UGC Roster (marketplace hire data): nano/micro creators commonly charge a **$70 flat fee per video**; mid-tier $100-$300. — [UGC Roster statistics](https://www.ugcroster.com/ugc-statistics)
- Other guides: $50-$100 for beginners ([UGCJobs](https://ugcjobs.com/blog/ugc-creator-salary)); $200-$400 including 90-day paid-ads usage ([Freelance Nation](https://freelancenation.com/blog/how-much-do-ugc-creators-charge)); $500-$1,200 for "professional" creators ([JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/ugc-creator-rates/)); micro-influencer TikTok $100-$500 per video ([Sideshift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-creator-rates-by-platform)). Usage rights add 25-150% ([Promote.sh](https://promote.sh/blog/content-creator-rate-guide)). One secondary source says creators with 50-100K followers average $800-$2,500 on TikTok Creator Marketplace (unverified).
- **Per-view / CPM programs (Whop Content Rewards):** third-party trackers put typical creator rates at ~$0.20-$6 per 1,000 views. Live campaign examples include $1.00/1K and $3.00/1K UGC briefs, and a Sep 5, 2026 snapshot showed $0.30 and $1.75/1K campaigns. Creators pay a 10% fee, and payouts run on a 7-day window plus a 3-day hold. Brand minimum budget **conflicts**: one guide says no published minimum; another says terms updated Sep 3, 2026 set a $1,000 minimum for CPM, per-post and retainer campaigns. — [Whop Content Rewards UGC listings](https://whop.com/discover/content-rewards/c/ugc/p/-2/); [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-clipping-guide.html); [Sprites.ai](https://www.sprites.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-guide); [FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/whop-clipping-pricing)
- Scale examples: Cal AI reportedly had **250 influencers on retainer** ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)). Turbo AI's creator network posted from 170+ accounts ([SGE](https://x.com/wesocialgrowth/status/2006410069710352812)). Both are self-reported or estimated.

**AI UGC avatar tools (third-party pricing; confirm on vendor sites)**
- Arcads: ~$110/mo Starter (about 10 videos, so roughly $11/video) and $220/mo Creator; Arcads doesn't publish pricing openly, and credit counts conflict. — [Eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/arcads-ai-pricing); [Wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/arcads-pricing)
- HeyGen: Free $0, Creator $29/mo ($24 annual), Pro $49, Business $149 + $20/seat (citing an Oct 2026 FAQ). — [Konabayev](https://konabayev.com/blog/heygen-pricing/); [Vuela](https://vuela.ai/alternative/heygen/pricing)
- Creatify: $39/mo monthly or $19/mo annual for 100 credits; Pro from $99/mo ($49 annual); entry price conflicts across sources ($29/$33). — [Camclo3d](https://camclo3d.com/blog/creatify-pricing); [Fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/creatify-ai-review-2026/)

**Platform AI-labeling rules**
- **TikTok (organic):** creators *must* label AI-generated or significantly edited content that looks realistic. Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted or labeled by TikTok (Community Guidelines version released Aug 25, 2026, effective Sep 24, 2026). — [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity). Creators switch on the "AI-generated content" setting or disclose via text, sticker or caption. TikTok may **auto-label** content made with its AI effects or carrying **C2PA Content Credentials**, and auto-labels can't be removed. TikTok says the label won't affect engagement if content is otherwise FYF-eligible. Misusing the label is a violation. — [TikTok Creator Academy: AI-generated content label](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label); [TikTok transparency: AI-generated content](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/supporting-responsible-transparent-ai-generated-content/). TikTok has labeled 1.3B+ videos. — [TikTok newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en)
- TikTok prohibits AI content showing fake authoritative sources, crisis events, or public figures in certain contexts, including false endorsements. Third-party compliance guides add that a minor's likeness, or an adult private person's likeness without permission, is barred even if labeled. — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity); [Novoads](https://novoads.ai/en/blog/tiktok-ai-ad-disclosure-rules)
- **TikTok ads:** third-party guides describe an AIGC disclosure toggle in Ads Manager for significantly AI-generated ads. Spark Ads follow the organic creator label. The Commercial Content Disclosure toggle is a separate requirement when promoting a product. Undisclosed AI ads detected later reportedly face rejection and account review (secondary sources; not confirmed on TikTok's Business Help Center). — [UGCVids](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/tiktok-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026); [Moveas](https://moveas.app/blog/tiktok-ads-ai-generated-content-label/)
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook):** "AI info" labels apply when Meta detects industry-standard AI indicators or when a user self-discloses. Users are expected to self-disclose photorealistic AI video and realistic audio, and Meta may penalize those who don't. Content only *edited* with AI gets the label in the post menu. — [Meta: Labeling AI content](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content/); [Meta newsroom, Apr 2024 (older)](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/). For ads, mandatory self-disclosure applies to social issue, election and political ads. Meta labels other ads made with its own generative tools depending on the edit: no label if the edits aren't significant and there is no photorealistic human. The search extract also mentions automated detection on ad media from June 1, 2026, with "AI Info" shown in "About this ad". — [Meta: GenAI transparency for ads (Feb 2025)](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/); [Meta SIEP ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/)
- **YouTube:** creators must disclose realistic altered or synthetic content. YouTube says the label does not affect recommendations or monetization; failing to disclose when required is the penalty trigger. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/no-new-rules-just-clearer-language-youtube-updates-monetisation-guidelines-74549.htm); [SOCH Fact Check](https://www.sochfactcheck.com/?p=18021)

### Inferences
- Stylized cartoon or Pixar-style animation is arguably not "realistic", so the mandatory-label rules bite hardest on **AI UGC avatars**: photorealistic humans giving fake testimonials. That makes AI avatar "testimonials" the riskiest format, for platform enforcement and possibly consumer-protection law. Stylized AI story videos are lower-risk. Labeling them voluntarily costs little, since TikTok and YouTube say labels don't reduce reach.
- Many AI tools embed C2PA metadata, so assume TikTok and Meta *will* detect and auto-label AI output. Plan creative that still works with an "AI-generated" tag.
- Budget fit: with $1K shared with development, the realistic paid-creator line is roughly $150-$300: 2-4 micro-UGC videos at ~$70 each, or a small Whop CPM campaign *if* the minimum is under the budget. Treat paid creators as a test of whether a human face beats the faceless and AI formats.

### Gaps
- Sideshift, Billo, Insense and TikTok One/Creator Marketplace pricing were not found from primary sources.
- TikTok Business Help Center text on the ad AIGC toggle was not read directly; ad-side rules come from third-party compliance blogs.
- The FTC (US) rules on fake or AI-generated testimonials were not researched in this pass and should be checked before using AI avatar "reviews".
- No data on how often AI-labeled app ads are rejected or throttled in practice.

## 4. App Store Optimization basics and launch tactics (Product Hunt, Reddit, TikTok search)

### Takeaway
ASO for a new app means fitting high-intent keywords into tight official limits. iOS: 30-character name, 30-character subtitle, 100-character keyword field. Google Play: 30-character title, 80-character short description. Launch-day tactics such as Product Hunt and Reddit give small, short-lived spikes, are better suited to tech or productivity audiences, and carry account-ban risk on Reddit. TikTok search optimization lets short-form content keep driving discovery after launch.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple App Store:** app name up to 30 characters; subtitle up to 30 characters (shown under the name); keyword field 100 characters (App Store Connect says 100 *bytes*, which matters for non-Latin scripts), comma-separated with no spaces. Don't repeat words already in the name or subtitle. Don't include category names, the word "app", or plurals of words already used. Irrelevant keywords or competitor names can cause rejection. — [Apple: Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/); [App Store Connect: App information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information/); [Apple: App Store search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- **Google Play:** app name 30-character limit (full- and half-width characters count the same); short description 80 characters, the first text users see. Avoid all-caps (unless it's the brand), emojis and irrelevant special characters in titles. Long titles may be truncated on some devices. — [Play Console Help: Metadata](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en); [Play Console Help: Store listing best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en)
- Apple Ads Basic gives a $100 credit to try (see section 5), which can double as cheap keyword-demand research. — [Apple Ads Basic overview](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/basic)
- **Product Hunt:** 2026 guides say it still works but penalizes low-engagement launches more than before and fits productivity, health and AI products. LaunchList calls non-technical consumers a poor audience fit. A launch agency estimates a top-5 finish yields ~500-3,000 installs (vendor estimate). The spike fades fast. — [LaunchList](https://getlaunchlist.com/blog/how-to-launch-on-product-hunt-2026); [The Viral App](https://theviralapp.com/blog/app-launch-strategy-2026/); [StartupBase](https://startupbase.io/blog/product-hunt-alternatives)
- **Reddit:** Quittr's founders posted heavily on Reddit after starting with ASO, and Reddit blocked many of their accounts. — [vc.ru](https://vc.ru/growth/1762617-kak-quittr-dorosli-do-35k-den)
- **TikTok search:** see section 1 for keywords in on-screen text, voiceover and caption.

### Inferences
- iOS keyword hygiene for a solo founder: put the highest-volume descriptive phrase in the subtitle (e.g., "Calorie counter & food scanner"), not just the brand. Use all 100 keyword characters with singular, non-duplicated terms. On Android, put the core keyword in the title and short description, since there is no keyword field.
- Screenshot best practice was not specifically sourced. The common approach (first two screenshots carry a benefit headline; reuse the best-performing short-form hook as screenshot 1 copy) is an inference linking organic hook testing to store conversion.
- For a consumer app or casual game, Product Hunt is low priority, except for productivity or AI-utility apps. Reddit is best used as genuine participation in niche subreddits, given the ban risk.

### Gaps
- No primary Apple or Google guidance on screenshot count and specs was retrieved in this pass (not searched due to tool budget).
- Reddit's current sitewide self-promotion rules were not found; check each subreddit's rules.
- No verified Product Hunt install numbers for consumer mobile apps.

## 5. Small paid tests: is a few hundred dollars on Meta / TikTok / Apple Ads useful?

### Takeaway
A few hundred dollars will not give statistically reliable install or subscription data on Meta or TikTok. TikTok's ad-group floor alone is >$20/day, and practitioners say conversion campaigns need far more to exit learning. It *can* serve as a creative-discovery or amplification test once organic shows a winner. Apple Ads Basic, with its $100 free credit, pay-per-install pricing and no minimum, is the most budget-compatible paid test for a new iOS app. Founders report mixed results.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Ads Basic:** pay only per install, up to a max CPI you set; Apple suggests a CPI from competitor data (help-doc example $0.60, illustrative only). Max budget is $10,000/month per app, with no minimum stated. Once the monthly budget is spent, ads stop until next month. Promo: try free with a **$100 credit**. — [Apple Ads: Promote an app with Basic](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/apple-ads-basic/0039-promote-an-app-with-basic); [Apple Ads Basic overview](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/basic); [Apple Ads: Compare solutions](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/apple-ads-basic/0001-compare-apple-ads-solutions)
- Benchmarks and founder reports (secondary): AppTweak 2025 US median CPI for search results ads is $4.06. One indie author tested Basic on three utility apps in Q1 2026, found Apple targeted broad terms with campaign-level reporting only, and 2 of 3 launches hit the CPI target within a week. A commonly cited r/AppStoreOptimization post describes spending $500 and getting back $149 (secondhand, unverified). Break-even example: 10% trial start × 40% trial-to-paid × $25.49 net/yr ≈ $1.02 revenue per install, so a CPI above that loses money in year one. — [Sonar: Apple Search Ads cost](https://trysonar.app/blog/apple-search-ads-cost); [Sonar guide](https://trysonar.app/blog/apple-search-ads-guide); [Adapty: Apple Ads benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/apple-ads-benchmarks-2026/)
- **TikTok Ads minimums (secondary sources, conflicting):** ad group daily budget must exceed $20. Campaign minimum is described variously as $50/day or $50 lifetime (or no limit). Guides say conversion-optimized US campaigns need ~$100-$200/day per ad group to exit learning, with $50-$150/day as a "minimum viable test". — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-ads-minimum-daily-budget-2026); [Coinis](https://coinis.com/how-to/minimum-budget-for-tiktok-ads); [Admanage](https://admanage.ai/blog/tiktok-ads-cost)
- **Meta:** guides conflict. One says $10-$20/day is workable for a solo test, with ~$150-$200 minimum ([Ad-vertly](https://www.ad-vertly.ai/post/meta-ads-budget-for-solo-founders)); another says an honest app test needs $5K-$10K over 30 days ([SEMNexus](https://semnexus.com/meta-ads-for-app-installs-the-2026-founder-playbook)). Adapty's framework: the **first $1K tests whether creative holds attention**, $1K-$10K proves the business, and paid only works once onboarding, paywall, pricing and retention are in order. — [Adapty: first $10K on Meta](https://adapty.io/blog/first-10k-meta-ads-subscription-apps/). A RevenueCat case used free trials as the optimization proxy event, given a 7-day attribution window. — [RevenueCat: high-velocity creative testing](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/high-velocity-creative-testing-framework-apps-meta)
- **Conversion benchmarks (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, 115K+ apps, mostly 2025 data):** trials of 17-32 days convert to paid at a median 42.5%, vs. 25.5% for trials under 4 days. Hard paywalls show a median Day-35 conversion of 10.7% vs. 2.1% for freemium. The extract calls this "trial-to-paid", but it is likely download-to-paid, so verify in the report. — [RevenueCat: subscription app benchmarks 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Organic should come first, and paid amplifies proven organic winners. — [RevenueCat Sub Club: Greg Stewart](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/sub-club-podcast-greg-stewart-ladder-fitness-app-growth); [Marcus Burke / RevenueCat masterclass](https://marcuscnslt.substack.com/p/revenuecat-meta-ads-masterclass)

### Inferences
- With ~$1K total shared with development, a sensible paid line is **$100-$250**: (a) claim the $100 Apple Ads Basic credit at launch to see keyword CPI and conversion; (b) optionally spend ~$100 boosting one proven organic winner (Spark Ads on TikTok, or boosting the Reel on Instagram) as an amplification check, not a scalable UA test. Skip TikTok conversion campaigns entirely at this budget.
- Use the Sonar-style break-even math before any spend: if expected revenue per install is around $1, a $4 CPI can't work. That pushes toward organic, ASO and paywall optimization first.

### Gaps
- No first-hand, primary-source founder post with exact numbers from a $100-$500 Meta or TikTok test in 2025-2026 was found.
- Meta's official minimum daily budget for app-install campaigns was not retrieved.
- Casual-game-specific CPI benchmarks were not gathered (game CPIs and monetization via ads or IAP differ from subscription apps).

## 6. Sample 30-day content calendar and weekly time budget for a solo founder

### Takeaway
No source gives a validated solo-founder calendar, so this section is mostly inference built on the cadences documented above. Stronger posted ~2.5 times a day; one thread describes 7+ a day on some accounts; Visualize AI succeeded with 2 accounts; Larry automated draft creation. A realistic target for a part-time solo founder is **1-2 slideshows a day across 2-3 accounts, plus 2-3 AI story videos a week**, in about **8-10 hours a week** using batching and scheduling.

### Cited Findings
- Cadence evidence: Stronger made 725 TikTok videos in 295 days (~2.5/day), 98% slideshows — [Shortimize](https://shortimize.com/blog/the-slideshow-strategy-that-generated-700000-users-in-275-days). One app's accounts posted 6,535 slideshows in ~1 year, some 7+/day — [Thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1830617819802992989). Visualize AI used 2 accounts — [SGE](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/niche-switch-how-this-ai-app-pulled-40m-views).
- Automation evidence: the Larry agent generates slideshows and uploads them as TikTok drafts for human review, with Postiz for scheduling — [Stack Junkie](https://www.stack-junkie.com/blog/how-to-automate-tiktok-marketing-with-openclaw-larry-skill-deep-dive); [Postiz blog](https://postiz.com/blog/automate-tiktok-slideshow-marketing-ai-agents-postiz). Slideshow generators (e.g., SlideStorm at 1 credit per slideshow, 3 per AI image) exist for batching — [SlideStorm](https://www.slidestorm.ai/).
- Iteration rule: rewrite only the hook and repost to isolate the cause — [Hooked](https://www.hooked.so/blog/how-to-create-a-tiktok-slideshow). Instagram Trial Reels allow testing on non-followers — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-new-trial-reels/).

### Inferences
**Sample 30-day calendar (inference; assumes the app is live by about day 8)**
- **Days -7 to 0 (pre-launch, ~10h total):** Set up 2 TikTok accounts and 1 Instagram professional account, plus YouTube Shorts on the same Google account. Use niche "theme" accounts, e.g. one problem-focused and one aesthetic/before-after. Each gets a unique email or phone and real browsing activity (warm-up best practice is undocumented). Write 30 hooks in 3 angles: problem/agitate, POV/story ("POV: you finally…"), listicle ("3 apps that…"). Produce 2-3 AI story videos. Finish ASO metadata.
- **Week 1 (launch):** 1 slideshow/day per TikTok account (14), each cross-posted as an Instagram carousel or Reel and a YouTube Short; 2 AI story videos; launch posts in 2-3 relevant subreddits where self-promotion is allowed; switch on the Apple Ads Basic $100 credit.
- **Week 2 (test):** Same volume, with every post a hook variant. Track 3-second view rate, completion, profile clicks and installs (UTM / store analytics). Use Instagram Trial Reels for riskier angles.
- **Week 3 (double down):** Kill the bottom 70% of angles. Make 5-10 remixes of the top 1-2 hooks (new visuals, same hook). Make an AI story version of the winning slideshow angle. Optionally hire 1-2 micro-UGC creators (~$70 each) on the winning angle.
- **Week 4 (amplify and review):** Keep cadence; boost the single best organic post with ~$50-$100. Update screenshot-1 copy to the winning hook. Retrospective: which format/angle drove installs, not just views. Decide whether to add a third account or a new niche angle.
- Target totals over 30 days: ~45-60 slideshows, ~8-10 AI story videos, 1-3 UGC videos, all cross-posted to 3 platforms.

**Weekly time budget (inference, ~8-10h/week)**
- Hook and script writing (batch): 1.5h
- Slideshow production with templates or a generator (~10-14 posts): 2-3h
- AI story videos (2-3/week: image keyframes, image-to-video, voiceover, CapCut edit): 3h
- Scheduling, posting and replying to comments (TikTok draft/scheduler workflow): 1-1.5h
- Metrics review and next-week plan: 0.5-1h

**Budget sketch within ~$1K shared with development (inference; prices per section 2)**
- One month of a video model (Kling Standard ~$9-10 or Google AI Pro $19.99) + ElevenLabs Starter $6 + CapCut free/Pro $19.99: ~$35-$50, then re-subscribe only in production months.
- Paid creators: $0-$200. Apple Ads: use the $100 credit, plus optional $0-$100. Boost: $50-$100.
- Total marketing outlay ≈ **$150-$450**, leaving the rest for developer accounts ($99/yr Apple; Google Play one-time fee, covered by other research notes) and tools.

### Gaps
- No primary source validates any specific solo posting cadence against outcomes. All cadences above come from self-reported winners (survivorship bias).
- Time-per-asset estimates are my inference, not sourced.
- Developer account fees and other development costs are outside this note's scope; the $99/yr Apple figure is common knowledge and was not re-verified here.
